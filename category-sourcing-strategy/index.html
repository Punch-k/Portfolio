CATEGORY SOURCING STRATEGY

The producer price index for corrugated boxes is up about 66% since January 2015. Buyers who negotiate on unit price alone miss where most of that cost comes from, and where most of the savings are.

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

The problem

Corrugated packaging is an indirect category whose price follows paperboard, energy, labor and diesel. Those inputs swing hard, and suppliers pass the swings on. Without a view of what a box should cost, buyers cannot tell a fair increase from an inflated one.

Why solve it

Packaging sits inside the landed cost of every product shipped. At Ward Tires, managing OEM procurement of about $350K a month on landed cost rather than unit price delivered a 20% landed cost reduction. The same discipline applies to any indirect category.

The answer

Run the category end to end: build a should-cost from public indexes, map spend, compare suppliers on total cost of ownership, score them, run a weighted RFQ, negotiate from the floor and walk-away, then make sure invoices match the contract. Award to two suppliers with index-linked pricing.

Why landed cost, not unit price
Unit price is only part of what a part costs to put on the shelf. Landed cost adds freight, handling, duty and the cost of holding stock. A lower price from a far away supplier can cost more once it lands. That is why every tool below works in total cost, not just price.

10. INSIGHTS

01

Box prices follow paperboard, so price should too.

The box index tracks the paperboard mills index closely. Linking contract price to that index replaces an annual argument with a formula both sides can check.

02

The cheapest bid is not the cheapest box.

Freight, damage, lead time and inventory can reverse a ranking based on unit price. Total cost of ownership is the only fair comparison.

03

Two suppliers buy resilience and leverage.

A primary and secondary award protects supply and keeps both suppliers competing for volume at each review.

04

Savings only count when invoices match.

Negotiated prices leak through off-contract buying and pricing errors. Compliance checks turn modeled savings into real ones.

Recommendation

Award a dual-source contract with a primary and secondary supplier. Price on a formula linked to the paperboard index, reviewed quarterly. Measure suppliers on a scorecard covering cost, quality, delivery and service. Audit invoices against the contract every month and recover overcharges.
