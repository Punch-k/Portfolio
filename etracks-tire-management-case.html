ETRACKS: THE 80% TIRE GAP

Ontario now requires tire producers to manage 80% of the tires they sell. After tread wear, that is about 91% of the scrap tires that actually exist. Today's collection network reaches about 88%.

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

The problem

In September 2026 Ontario raised the tire management target from 65% to 80%, effective for the 2026 year, and removed the $1 million cap on penalties. eTracks, the producer responsibility organization (PRO) behind roughly 70% of the market, now has to collect and process more tires through a network that already backs up every spring and fall. Dealers estimated 750,000 to 1 million tires waiting for pickup by July 2026.

Why solve it

If the network stays as it is, the model misses the target in every year from 2027 to 2030. By December 2030, 1.4M tires are left sitting at sites, 22,000 t have gone to private haulers, and penalty exposure reaches $13.4M with no cap. Stockpiled tires are a fire and pest risk, and dealers pay twice when they have to call a private hauler.

The answer

A Balanced plan meets the target in all four years and clears the backlog by the end of 2027. It adds sites, peak-season surge hauling, 128,000 t of processing and call-in pickups, and keeps hauling after the target is met. The cost is about $0.40 more per tire supplied. Processing capacity becomes the next constraint, and the market-wide gap is evidence to take to the regulator.

I. THE MANDATE

Ontario runs tire recycling through extended producer responsibility. Under the Tires Regulation (O. Reg. 225/18), producers must manage a share of the tires they supply into the province. Most meet that obligation through a PRO, which runs collection sites, hires haulers and pays processors. eTracks is the largest PRO, with roughly 70% of the market.4

Jan 2025

The target drops from 85% to 65%. Several PROs slow or stop collecting once they hit it, and the number of required sites falls to about 4,450.34

Mar 2026

Spring volumes run 43% above normal heading into March. A processor stops accepting loads for days after a PRO volume quota, and haulers warn of disruption.4

Jul 2026

The Ontario Tire Dealers Association estimates 750,000 to 1 million tires waiting for collection across the province.3

Sep 2026

The amendments are finalized. The target rises to 80% for 2026 and every year after, with no pro-rating. Required sites rise from about 4,300 to about 6,500 from 2027, with 220 of the new sites in Northern Ontario. The $1 million cap on administrative penalties is removed.12

The question for a PRO is no longer whether the target is reasonable. The questions are how big the physical gap is, where in the network tires get lost, and which plan closes the gap at an acceptable cost. I built a 60-month model to answer all three.

"An 80% target on tires sold is a 91% target on tires scrapped."

Model finding, 2028

II. THE GAP

The target is measured against the three-year average weight of tires supplied. But about 15% of that weight wears off as tread before a tire is scrapped, and the market keeps growing. So 80% of supply works out to 91.4% of the scrap that is actually generated.

A collection site can only take tires from the generators near it. In the model, today's network reaches 88.1% of scrap, weighted by where scrap is generated. Before any backlog, leakage or capacity limit, that caps the highest target the network could meet at about 77%. The market-wide shortfall is about 6,000 t a year, or roughly 550,000 tires that no amount of hauling can reach.

Market-wide, all PROs. 2025 used the 65% target, so collectable tonnes exceeded the requirement. From 2026 the requirement sits above what today's network can reach.

III. WHERE TIRES GET STUCK

The model follows eTracks' 70% share month by month: tires generated, reached, accepted at a site, hauled, and processed. Losses come from four places. Select one.

Reach follows diminishing returns. Each region's reach rises with site count but levels off. The North holds 9% of scrap, has the longest hauls ($260/t) and reaches only 68% today. Sites alone cannot get it to 95%. Getting it to 90% would take about 2,300 sites, against 800 today. Call-in pickups for generators with 50 or more tires add about 3 points of reach for a $12/t premium, which is cheaper than building that density.

Tires arrive in two waves, but hauling is flat. Generation runs at 0.55 times the monthly average in January and 1.55 times in April, with a second peak in October and November when drivers change to winter tires. A hauling budget spread evenly at 9,000 t a month falls behind every March to May and every September to November, and the backlog never fully clears. The model's profile matches the 43% jump in volumes reported heading into March 2026.4

Long waits push tires out of the system. Once tires sit at a site for more than about three weeks, dealers start calling private haulers. In the model, leakage rises from zero at 21 days to a 15% maximum share of new tires at 66 days, or 22% in the stress test. Tires that go to private haulers never count toward the target, and dealers pay for removal after the fee was already collected at the point of sale. Dealer groups have raised exactly this concern with the province.4 On the Flat budget, 22,000 t leak out between 2027 and 2030.

Processing is the next bottleneck. The five processors in the model have 168,000 t of capacity, but 24% of it goes to tires imported from the US, leaving 128,250 t for Ontario. The Balanced plan contracts 128,000 t, which uses 99.8% of what is left. Processors refuse loads once their yards hold two months of stock, so an outage or more imports blocks hauling upstream. If import share fell to 15% at every processor, 17,250 t of capacity would free up. Higher targets do not create processing capacity on their own.5

IV. THREE PLANS

Each scenario runs its own calculation engine over 2027 to 2030. Flat budget keeps today's network: 6,500 sites, 108,000 t of hauling, 112,000 t of processing, and the 2025 habit of slowing hauling once the target is met. Balanced plan adds 1,300 sites, a 125,000 t hauling base with 40% surge capacity in the six peak months, 128,000 t of processing and call-in pickups, and it does not pause. Stress test keeps today's network but adds sharper peaks, more leakage and 8% processing outages.

VI. INSIGHTS

01

It is a reach problem before it is a hauling problem.

Below 91% reach, no hauling budget can deliver 80% of supply. The first dollars should go to sites and call-in pickups, which together lift reach from 88% to 95%. The North needs depots and call-ins, not more drop-off sites.

02

The backlog comes from a seasonal mismatch, not a shortage of total capacity.

Over a year, hauling of 108,000 t is close to what reaches a site. The problem is timing: generation nearly triples from January to April while hauling stays flat. Surge capacity in the six peak months, about 17,000 t a year, does more than a bigger flat budget would.

03

Year-end carryover is the hidden cost.

Even before the new target applies, flat hauling leaves 868,000 tires waiting at sites at the end of 2026, so every year starts behind. The Balanced plan clears that carryover in its first year and keeps it at zero each December. It also drops the 2025 habit of slowing hauling once the target is met. That rule never triggers on the Flat budget, because the Flat budget never reaches the target. Under any plan that does reach it, though, a pause would rebuild the year-end pile behind the 2025 backlog.

04

Processing headroom is the plan's weak point.

At 99.8% utilization, an 8% outage or one processor taking more imports puts the plan short again. Contracts should include about 10% headroom, priority access for Ontario tires, and a Northern outlet such as pyrolysis to cut the $260/t haul.

05

At $300/t, paying the penalty is cheaper. That will not last.

Including penalties, the Flat budget is $22.4M cheaper than the Balanced plan, and compliance only pays for itself above $801 per tonne short. But the cap is gone, and PRO financial reporting now feeds into penalty calculations, so the regulator can set penalties to make non-compliance irrational. Unpriced costs also favour compliance: stockpile fire risk, double fees for dealers and loss of trust.

Recommendation

Adopt the Balanced plan in three steps. Q1 2027: add sites and switch on call-in pickups to lift reach. Before March 2027: contract surge hauling for the spring wave and end the pause rule. During 2027: renegotiate processing for about 10% headroom. In parallel, give RPRA the gap analysis: on today's network, the highest target that can be met is about 77% of supply, not 80%.
