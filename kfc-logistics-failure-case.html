KFC'S LOGISTICS FAILURE

On 14 February 2018 KFC UK moved restaurant deliveries from six Bidvest depots to a single DHL depot in Rugby. Within five days, around 575 of its roughly 900 restaurants in the UK and Ireland were closed.

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

The problem

KFC switched its UK logistics contract to DHL, which ran fresh-chicken distribution through one depot in Rugby instead of the six Bidvest had used. The new operation could not handle the volume from day one, and deliveries were missed or incomplete.

Why solve it

With no backup depot, every failure at Rugby reached the restaurants directly. On the evening of 18 February only 266 of 870 restaurants were open, and more than 470 were still closed on 20 February. Each closed day lost sales and spoiled fresh stock.

The answer

Treat any 3PL switch for a perishable network as a resilience test, not a cost exercise. Run old and new networks in parallel, cut over region by region, keep the incumbent on standby, and set go or no-go gates on staffing and fill rate before each step.

I. THE SWITCH

KFC UK moved its logistics contract from Bidvest Logistics to DHL, with DHL taking over on 14 February 2018.15 DHL's model consolidated fresh-chicken distribution into one depot in Rugby, replacing a network of six warehouses.1 On paper it was a simpler, cheaper network. In practice it removed every backup the old network had.

II. THE SINGLE POINT OF FAILURE

Select a workstream to see how the failure compounded.

A go-live problem. DHL said operational issues meant a number of deliveries had been incomplete or delayed.5 Fresh chicken has a short shelf life, so a depot that falls behind cannot catch up by working through a backlog later.

No reroute available. With one depot serving the whole country, a problem at Rugby became a problem in every restaurant. Under the old six-depot network, a failure at one site could have been covered by the others.23

V. INSIGHTS

01

Cost per depot is visible; the value of redundancy is not.

Cutting from six depots to one looks efficient until the day one site fails. Redundancy has to be priced as insurance in the business case.

02

Perishables leave no room to recover.

A late delivery of dry goods is a delay; a late delivery of fresh chicken is waste and a closed restaurant.

03

Big-bang cutovers concentrate risk.

Switching the whole country on one date meant every restaurant felt the go-live problems at once.

04

Recovery speed and honest communication matter.

KFC rebuilt supply over the following days and was open about the failure, which limited the damage to the brand.

Recommendation

For any 3PL transition on a perishable network: run in parallel with the incumbent for several weeks; cut over by region, not nationally; keep the incumbent on standby until the new network hits agreed fill-rate targets; and set go or no-go gates on staffing, system readiness and on-time delivery before each step.
