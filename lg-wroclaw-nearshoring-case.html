LG WROCLAW

LG Energy Solution builds EV batteries near Wroclaw, about 450 to 950 km from the headquarters of the European automakers it supplies, instead of shipping them from its home plant in Korea, more than 8,000 km away.

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

The problem

European automakers need very large volumes of EV batteries. Importing cells from Asia means long lead times, costly freight for a heavy and hazardous product, and weaker alignment with EU content rules.

Why solve it

The battery is the most expensive part of an EV. A supplier that cannot deliver reliably and close to the assembly line puts the whole vehicle program at risk.

The answer

Build where the customers are. LG put a fully integrated battery complex near Wroclaw, Poland: more than €3 billion invested on about 100 hectares, with more than 9,500 employees and capacity reported between 70 and 86 GWh, scaling toward 100 GWh.

I. WHY POLAND

LG Chem, now operating as LG Energy Solution, chose Kobierzyce near Wroclaw in southwestern Poland for its European battery plant. The site sits in a special economic zone on about 100 hectares and has expanded in stages.12 LG describes it as the largest battery plant in Europe.5

Wroclaw is within trucking distance of the German and Central European car industry, which is where the batteries are installed. Its customers include Audi, BMW, Fiat, Ford, Porsche and Volkswagen.2

"Proximity has a price. So does distance."

II. FULL INTEGRATION

The Wroclaw complex produces every stage of the battery, from electrodes to cells, modules and finished packs.34 Select a workstream.

Built in stages. Reported capacity varies by date and source, from about 70 GWh to about 86 GWh, with a stated target of 100 GWh once the latest expansion stage is complete.56 Building in phases ties each investment to confirmed automaker demand rather than one large bet.

Beyond passenger EVs. The plant has added batteries for electric buses and energy storage, spreading its demand across more than one market.3

V. INSIGHTS

01

Distance is a cost even when it is not on the invoice.

Weeks of transit tie up working capital and slow down engineering changes. Being a day away lets LG and its customers fix problems quickly.

02

Phased capacity limits the downside.

Building in stages ties each investment to confirmed demand, which matters as EV adoption in Europe moves unevenly.

03

Customer concentration is both the reason and the risk.

The plant exists to serve a cluster of German and European automakers. A slowdown in that cluster hits the plant directly, which is why it has added bus and storage batteries.

04

Local production supports EU content rules.

Batteries made in the EU fit more easily with rules and incentives that favor local content.

Recommendation

Keep scaling in phases tied to signed automaker volumes. Diversify demand further into energy storage to offset swings in EV sales. Localize inputs such as cathode and anode materials to match the plant's proximity advantage. Measure it: track delivered cost per kWh and lead time against imported cells, not unit manufacturing cost alone.
