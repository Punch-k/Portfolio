LG WROCLAW

Evaluating strategic nearshoring: how LG built Europe's largest EV battery gigafactory in Poland instead of importing cells from Asia.

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

LG Energy Solution's complex near Wrocław, Poland (Kobierzyce commune / Biskupice Podgórne) is the largest lithium-ion EV battery factory in Europe, with roughly 70 GWh of current capacity scaling toward 100 GWh — fully integrated from electrodes through cells, modules, and packs, supplying automakers including Audi, BMW, Fiat, Ford, Porsche, and Volkswagen. This case examines the nearshoring logic: why a South Korean battery maker chose to build its most important European capacity on the ground, close to its automotive customers, instead of shipping finished cells across continents.

I. WHY POLAND

LG Chem (now largely operated under LG Energy Solution) chose Kobierzyce, near Wrocław in southwestern Poland, as the site for its flagship European EV battery plant. The site sits inside the EURO-PARK KOBIERZYCE special economic zone, on roughly 100 hectares, and has expanded in stages toward a targeted 100 GWh of capacity — enough to make it, on completion, the largest EV battery factory in the world.

Poland itself is the EU's largest exporter of car batteries, and Wrocław sits within efficient trucking distance of the German, and broader Central European, automotive manufacturing belt — the actual customers for the batteries being produced.

"A battery gigafactory built next to its customers isn't just cheaper to ship from — it's a different, faster feedback loop with the automaker's own engineering teams."

— Regional Supply Strategy Note

II. FULL VERTICAL INTEGRATION

The Wrocław complex was built as a fully integrated production system — producing all battery components from electrodes through cells, modules, and finished packs, the first time this had been done at this scale in Europe. Select a workstream below.

Staged Expansion: Capacity has been built out in stages toward a targeted 100 GWh, rather than committed all at once — matching capital deployment to confirmed automaker demand rather than betting the full investment on a single forecast.

Beyond EV Batteries: The Wrocław operation has expanded output to include e-bus batteries and energy-storage-system cells, diversifying the plant's demand base beyond passenger EVs alone — a hedge against any single segment's demand slowing.

III. THE AUTOMAKER PROXIMITY THESIS

Batteries produced at Wrocław supply electric vehicles for Audi, BMW, Fiat, Ford, Porsche, and Volkswagen — a customer list that is, not coincidentally, concentrated in exactly the geography the plant sits closest to. Building at scale near the customer reduces logistics cost and lead time on a heavy, hazardous-to-ship product, and it also enables tighter, faster collaboration on next-generation cell chemistry between LG's engineers and each automaker's platform teams.

IV. THE NEARSHORING TRADE-OFF

Building Europe's largest battery gigafactory in Poland instead of shipping cells from Asia trades a lower nominal unit-manufacturing cost for lower logistics cost, faster lead time, EU regulatory and content alignment, and a much tighter feedback loop with automaker customers. It's the same strategic calculus every nearshoring decision runs — proximity has a price, but so does distance, and the Wrocław investment is a bet that proximity to a concentrated automotive customer base wins that trade-off at gigafactory scale.
