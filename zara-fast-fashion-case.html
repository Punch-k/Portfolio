CAN ZARA FAST-FORWARD FASHION

Inditex nearly doubled sales from €20.4 billion in its pandemic year to €39.9 billion in FY2025. Its edge is a supply chain that makes much of its fashion close to home, in small batches, and reacts to what sells within weeks.

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

The problem

Growth is slowing. Inditex sales rose 3.2% in FY2025, or 7.0% at constant currency, after double-digit years. Competitors are closing the speed gap with better forecasting, and tariffs and EU textile rules are adding cost and complexity.

Why solve it

Speed is the business model. Owning design, production and distribution lets Zara put new items in stores in two to three weeks and restock twice a week. If speed stops paying, the cost of running factories with spare capacity becomes hard to justify.

The answer

Keep proximity production for trend-driven items, where speed earns full price, and source basics further away. Use store and online sales data to cut first-order sizes further, and invest the savings from fewer markdowns in flexible capacity.

I. THE INTEGRATION MODEL

Where most apparel brands outsource design, manufacturing and logistics to separate vendors, Inditex controls most of it for Zara, coordinated by a central IT system that links store sales back to design and factory scheduling.12 It also keeps spare capacity in its own factories on purpose, which looks wasteful on a cost model but buys the ability to react within days.

A large share of production stays close to its Spanish headquarters. At the end of 2023 about 48% of Inditex's suppliers were in the proximity countries of Spain, Portugal, Morocco and Turkey.610

"Every competitor can copy the clothes. Few will run factories below capacity on purpose."

Case thesis

II. THE QUICK-RESPONSE ENGINE

Zara can design, produce and deliver new items to stores in as little as two to three weeks, with stores restocked twice a week.13 Select a lever below.

Short lead times over low unit cost. Producing in Spain, Portugal, Morocco and Turkey costs more per unit than far-shore sourcing but cuts lead time from months to weeks. In 2023 Morocco had the most Inditex suppliers of the group's sourcing markets, ahead of Turkey and Portugal.610

A defense against the bullwhip effect. Small first orders, refilled from real sales, keep production close to actual demand instead of a seasonal forecast. That reduces both stockouts and the end-of-season markdowns that erode margin in fashion.2

III. THE NUMBERS

Inditex sales fell to €20.4 billion in FY2020 and recovered to €27.7 billion the next year.78 FY2025 brought a record €39.9 billion in sales and €6.2 billion in net income, with Spain at 15.9% of sales.5 Growth, though, has slowed from double digits to low single digits in euro terms.

V. INSIGHTS

01

Speed pays only where trends move fast.

Proximity production earns its higher unit cost on trend items that sell at full price. On basics, slower and cheaper sourcing makes more sense.

02

Spare capacity is the real moat.

Competitors can copy designs in weeks. Few are willing to keep factories below full use so they can react.

03

Better forecasting narrows the gap.

As rivals use AI forecasting, the value of reacting faster than the forecast shrinks. Zara has to keep turning sales data into smaller, more accurate first orders.

04

Regulation adds cost to the whole industry.

EU textile and waste rules raise costs for everyone, and a model built on fewer markdowns and less unsold stock is better placed than most.

Recommendation

Segment sourcing: keep trend-driven lines in proximity markets and move stable basics further afield. Shrink first orders: use store and online sales data to cut initial buys and refill faster. Fund flexibility: reinvest markdown savings in spare capacity. Track it: report full-price sell-through and markdown rate by sourcing region.
