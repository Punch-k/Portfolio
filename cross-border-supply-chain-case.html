CROSS-BORDER SUPPLY CHAIN

Ward Tires — Calgary Distribution Centre. Stripping out safety stock in a market where a single Ontario-to-Alberta storm can wipe out shelf availability.

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Ward Tires' Calgary DC carried heavy safety stock as insurance against a volatile, weather-exposed Ontario supply line. We rebuilt the vendor development strategy around tighter, JIT-style replenishment cycles instead of buffer inventory — cutting stockouts 10-15% during peak season while reducing the working capital tied up in tires that were simply waiting. The EOQ and safety-stock math behind this decision is fully interactive in the site's Inventory Lab.

Date: February 2026

Read Time: 5 Minutes

Focus: Supply Chain, Vendor Strategy, Risk

I. THE CALCULATED RISK

Ward Tires moved roughly 12,000 units annually of its top-selling all-season SKU through a Calgary distribution centre supplied out of Ontario, with an average 21-day lead time that swung between 14 and 28 days depending on weather and carrier congestion. In a market where winter tire demand spikes hard and fast, the instinctive response is to hold more safety stock. We proposed the opposite: strip safety stock down and rebuild the supply line so it didn't need the buffer in the first place.

That's a genuine commercial risk in a category where a stockout during the first cold snap of the season means a lost sale to a competitor down the street, not a backorder a customer will patiently wait on.

"Cutting inventory isn't a supply chain move. It's a bet that your vendor relationship is strong enough to replace the warehouse."

— Vendor Strategy Review

II. THE VENDOR SHIFT

Rather than cutting inventory in isolation, we restructured the upstream relationship first. Select a workstream below.

Joint Forecasting: We worked directly with the supplier's R&D and production planning teams to share demand signal earlier and more granularly — shifting from a purely transactional PO relationship to a collaborative forecasting one, which is what actually shrinks effective lead time variability.

EOQ Re-Baseline: Ordering cost (~$150/order), a 25% annual holding cost rate, and a 95% target fill rate were re-run through the EOQ and safety-stock formulas with the new, tighter lead-time variance the vendor relationship enabled — justifying a materially smaller reorder point without giving up service level.

III. WEATHER & TARIFF EXPOSURE

Two risk factors sit on top of ordinary demand variability in this lane: winter weather disruption on the Ontario-to-Alberta corridor, and cross-border tariff/rules-of-origin exposure on any imported tire components. Both were modeled as lead-time variance inputs rather than treated as unknowable black swans — which is precisely what let the safety-stock formula do its job instead of defaulting to gut-feel buffer stock.

IV. THE OUTCOME

Shelf availability during peak season improved by 10-15%, even with a smaller physical inventory footprint — proof that the safety-stock formula and the vendor relationship it depends on are really the same lever, not two separate ones. The distribution centre freed up working capital that had been sitting in tires, not sales.
